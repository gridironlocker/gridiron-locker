Six hundred and ninety-three days. That is how long Deshaun Watson went between NFL starts — one torn Achilles in October 2024, a second during rehab, a lost 2025, and then a training-camp win over the quarterback half of Cleveland actually wanted to watch. His return lasted about a quarter and a half. The Browns were beaten 34-10 in Jacksonville on Sunday, and by Monday morning nobody in Cleveland was talking about the score any more. They were talking about the roster spot above it.

Quick answer: is Shedeur Sanders starting Sunday?

  • Who starts Week 2: Deshaun Watson. Head coach Todd Monken said on Monday, September 14 that he anticipates Watson starting Sunday, September 20 at Tampa Bay (1:00 PM ET, CBS, Raymond James Stadium).
  • How firm: not firm. Monken confirmed the Week 2 plan after watching tape; he did not close the season. "We've got to play better around him. We've got to call it better, and he's got to play better," he said, per Newsweek.
  • What just happened: the Browns lost 34-10 to Jacksonville. Watson threw an interception on the opening drive, was sacked five times, and finished 16-for-22 for 205 yards with one touchdown, a 97.7 rating and 38 rushing yards — which led the team.
  • What comes next: ESPN's Adam Schefter reported the original plan was to run Watson through the first two games — both on the road — and re-evaluate after that. The pressure date is the home opener, Sunday, September 27 against Carolina.

What actually happened in Jacksonville

The damage was done before the Browns ever had a chance to settle in. Watson's third pass of the season was intercepted by Foye Oluokun and the opening drive was over before it started. Cleveland trailed 24-0 at the half. By the time the box score was kind, the game had been decided for a while: Watson's 46-yard touchdown to rookie wide receiver Denzel Boston came with 1:52 left.

The individual line is not a disaster — 72.7% completion, 9.3 yards per attempt, one turnover. The context is. He was on the field for the first regular-season start since October 20, 2024 against Cincinnati (Beacon Journal), the offensive line gave up five sacks, and Cleveland committed 11 penalties to Jacksonville's four, most of them on offence (Cleveland.com). Watson turned 31 the day after the game.

The scoreboard also carried a small historical oddity that matters here: Cleveland opened a season with two straight road games for the first time since 1986, per the club's schedule release. That is two weeks of a quarterback decision being made in public, with no home crowd in the building for either one.

Why Monken refuses to make this one man's problem

Monken's postgame position, repeated Monday, is that benching the quarterback would be a shortcut. He said it was "unfair" to place the loss on Watson alone and that he would "evaluate everything" (CBS Sports).

Fair or not, there are three structural facts behind the caution, and a fan is better off knowing them than arguing without them:

  1. The protection, not just the pocket. Five sacks against a Jacksonville front that was built to rush. Change the quarterback and that number does not automatically move.
  2. The operation. Eleven penalties, several pre-snap, on offence. This is a team that has not finished a drive cleanly, whoever is under centre.
  3. The money. Watson is on a five-year, $230 million fully guaranteed contract signed after the 2022 trade; Pro Football Rumors notes he is on a path toward a post-June-1 release designation next spring rather than a clean cut. Benching is free. The next step is not.

Is Shedeur Sanders actually ready — or is this wishful thinking?

Both. That is the honest answer, and it is why this argument has legs beyond one bad Sunday.

The case for him is not the highlights. It is that Cleveland's rookie-year finish happened with him: three wins to close 2025, including a 364-yard game against Tennessee — the third-most passing yards by a Browns rookie in a single game that season (ClevelandBrowns.com).

The case against rushing him is messier than people want it to be. He completed 56.6% of his passes as a rookie and held the ball longer than anyone in the league last season, which is a protection problem and a processing problem at the same time. A fifth-round pick who inherits an offensive line that just gave up five sacks to Jacksonville is not obviously the fix — and Monken has already said the quiet part about the 2025 quarterback room by refusing to treat the job as week-to-week.

What makes this more than fan noise is where it crossed over. Within 18 hours of the final score, the conversation had moved from Cleveland message boards to a New York Times column asking how long the franchise will keep absorbing the cost of its Watson era. That is the shape of a real trend, not a bad afternoon.

The fan argument, accurately described

One thing worth naming, because it is doing a lot of the work here: this fanbase is not hostile to Watson, it is exhausted. He told reporters he considered the boos during the preseason loss to Buffalo disrespectful and would keep his feelings about it private, per NFL.com. He was booed by his own home crowd in August, then handed the job anyway. Cleveland fans have lived through the "big swing and miss" owner's admission, the suspension, the injuries and now the 693 days. The Dawg Pound's default position is not outrage. It is arithmetic — how much longer, and for what.

Gridiron Locker's own Fan Trend Index, which counts how often each name appears in the headlines across all four fanbases we cover, had Watson at 50 mentions in the last 10 days — the single hottest name on the board, ahead of Michigan's Bryce Underwood (16) and Dallas' Dak Prescott (6). Sanders sits third at 12. The gap between those two numbers is precisely the argument.

Frequently asked questions

Is Deshaun Watson the Browns' starting quarterback right now?

Yes. Todd Monken said on September 14, 2026 that he anticipates Watson starting Week 2 against Tampa Bay after reviewing the Week 1 tape. It is a start, not a season-long announcement.

When does Shedeur Sanders start for the Browns?

No date has been given. ESPN's Adam Schefter reported that Cleveland's plan entering the season was Watson for the first two games — both on the road — and a re-evaluation after that, which puts the first realistic window around the September 27 home opener against Carolina.

How did Deshaun Watson do in Week 1 against Jacksonville?

16 completions on 22 attempts (72.7%) for 205 yards, one touchdown, one interception and five sacks taken, for a 97.7 passer rating. He also led the Browns with 38 rushing yards. Cleveland lost 34-10 and fell behind 24-0 in the first half.

Why not just bench Watson after one bad game?

Because the loss was not only a quarterback loss: Cleveland was flagged 11 times and gave up five sacks. Monken also has a fully guaranteed contract to consider — Watson's five-year, $230 million deal makes a clean release difficult, with a post-June-1 designation the realistic mechanism next spring.

What are Shedeur Sanders' Browns stats?

In eight games and seven starts in 2025 he completed 56.6% of his passes for 1,400 yards with seven touchdowns and 10 interceptions, added 169 rushing yards, and went 3-4 — finishing the season on a three-game winning run as a starter.

Who is the Browns' backup quarterback in 2026?

Shedeur Sanders and Dillon Gabriel entered the season as the backups behind Watson, after Sanders pushed the job in camp and preseason.

When do the Browns play their home opener in 2026?

Sunday, September 27 against the Carolina Panthers at Huntington Bank Field, 1:00 PM ET on FOX — the latest home opener in franchise history since 1975, because Cleveland opens on the road at Jacksonville and Tampa Bay.

Are the Browns playing Baker Mayfield in Week 2?

Yes. Cleveland travels to Raymond James Stadium on September 20 to face Tampa Bay, which lost its own opener at Cincinnati by six points.
